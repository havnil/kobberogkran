A bathroom is a big investment, and most of what decides whether it stays watertight ends up behind the tiles. Eirik does the plumbing for your bathroom and coordinates with the electrician and tiler, so you have one person to deal with from the site visit to the finished documentation.

Why no bathroom price online?

No two bathrooms are alike. A small bathroom with pipes in good condition is a completely different job from one where the drain has to be rerouted and the floor built up. A price online would either be too high for most people or too low for many. That is why you get a written offer after a site visit, with a fixed price or an estimate for the plumbing.

What the plumber does in a bathroom

  • New water and drain pipes, and rerouting where fixtures move
  • The floor drain and its connection to the membrane, done to current standards
  • Fitting the toilet, basin, shower and mixer taps
  • The water heater and washing machine if they are in the bathroom
  • Photos of everything hidden, and documentation of the wet room work

What you can do before the site visit

  • Take photos of the bathroom as it is today, including the drain and where the pipes come in.
  • Roughly measure the floor, and note the ceiling height.
  • Think about what you want: shower or bath, wall-hung toilet, underfloor heating.
  • If you live in a housing co-operative or owners’ association, check with the board what needs approval.

How it works

  1. Request with photos and measurements

    Send photos of the bathroom as it is today, rough measurements and what you would like. If you live in a housing co-operative, check with the board early.

  2. Site visit

    Eirik looks at the bathroom, the pipes and the drain, and what it takes to make the room watertight and up to current standards.

  3. Written offer

    You get a written offer for the plumbing, as a fixed price or an estimate, stating who does what if an electrician and a tiler are involved.

  4. Plan and start date

    You agree on a start date and the order of work. Eirik coordinates with the other tradespeople, so you don't have to be the project manager.

  5. Work and checks

    The pipes go in, the membrane and drain are checked, and everything that will be hidden is photographed before the walls are closed.

  6. Finished and documented

    The fittings are installed and tested. You get FDV documentation and documentation of the plumbing work in the wet room.

Eirik is responsible to you

When an electrician and a tiler work through Kobber og Kran, your agreement is with Eirik. You have one person to call and one person to deal with if something is not as agreed. If you bring your own tradespeople, you agree in writing who is responsible for what before work starts.

Common questions

What does a new bathroom cost?

It depends on the size, what is there today, what you want and how much has to be torn out. That is why there is no bathroom price here. After a site visit you get a written offer with a fixed price or an estimate for the plumbing.

Do you do the whole bathroom?

Eirik does the plumbing: water, drains, the floor drain and fitting the fixtures. An electrician and a tiler can work through Kobber og Kran, and then Eirik coordinates the work and is responsible to you. If you bring your own tradespeople, that works too, but responsibility must be agreed in writing before work starts.

Why does documentation matter?

A bathroom that is built properly and documented is easier to sell, and your insurer will ask for it if something happens. Eirik photographs everything hidden in walls and floors, and you get FDV documentation and documentation of the plumbing work in the wet room.

I live in a housing co-operative. What should I think about?

Most housing co-operatives and owners' associations require the board to approve the bathroom before work starts, and some have their own requirements for plumbers and documentation. Check with the board early and send what they require, so Eirik can take it into account in the offer.

How long does it take?

A typical bathroom takes several weeks from demolition to finish, because membrane, tiles and grout need time to dry. You get a plan with the order of work before the start. How soon Eirik can start depends on what he has already agreed.

Do I have to pay anything up front?

For bathrooms it is common to agree that materials ordered specially for your bathroom are paid up front or along the way. This is always stated in the written offer, so you know before you say yes.

Do you have wet room approval?

Approvals are shown on the About Eirik page once they are in place. Feel free to ask what applies to your bathroom.

    Leaking right now?

    1. Turn off the main stopcock. It is usually where the water comes into the house: in the cellar, the utility room, under the kitchen sink or by the water heater. If you have a well, you can also switch off the pump.
    2. Switch off the power to the water heater at the fuse box if the tank is leaking or the water is turned off.
    3. Mop up what you can, and take photos of the damage and of what is leaking.
    4. Report it to your insurer. Most have their own emergency service that can come and dry things out.
    5. Call Eirik.

    Eirik takes urgent jobs when he can, but Kobber og Kran does not have a 24-hour emergency service. If he can't answer, your insurer's emergency service is the quickest help.
