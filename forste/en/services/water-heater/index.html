A leaking water heater is one of the most common causes of water damage in Norwegian homes. If your tank is old, it is cheaper to replace it before it leaks than after. Kobber og Kran replaces water heaters at a fixed price for the work when the new tank goes in the same place as the old one.

A fixed price, but for the work only

The fixed price covers what is the same from job to job: draining and disconnecting the old tank, fitting the new one, connecting the water, pressure testing and taking the old one away. The tank itself and parts are extra. That is why we also show a typical total, so you can see what a replacement usually costs all in.

If something isn’t as it should be, for example the tank has to be moved or there is no floor drain, you are told, with the price, before Eirik starts.

What Eirik needs from you

  • A photo of the rating plate on the tank. It shows size, power and age.
  • A photo of the tank and the floor around it, so he can see the drain, any leak stop and the space.
  • Which room and which floor the tank is on.
  • Whether the tank is plugged into a socket or hard-wired.

You can add all of this in the form at the bottom of the page. In most cases Eirik can then give you a price without a site visit.

How the replacement goes

  1. Eirik turns off the water and the power to the tank, and drains the old one.
  2. The old tank is disconnected and carried out.
  3. The new one goes in with a safety valve and a mixing valve.
  4. The water is connected, and everything is pressure tested and checked for leaks.
  5. You get FDV documentation for the new tank, and the old one is taken away.

When should the tank be replaced?

  • It is around 20 years old or more.
  • There is rust or damp around the connections or under the tank.
  • The hot water runs out faster than before, or the water comes out rusty.
  • The safety valve drips all the time.

Common questions

What is included in the fixed price?

The fixed price covers the work of replacing a standard water heater in the same place: draining and disconnecting the old one, fitting the new one with the valves that go with it, connecting the water, pressure testing and taking the old one away. The tank itself and parts are extra. The typical total is shown next to the fixed price, so you can see what it usually comes to.

What isn't included?

Moving the tank somewhere else, a new floor drain or leak protection to current standards, an electrician if the tank is hard-wired, and extra work where access is difficult. If Eirik can see from the photos that any of this is needed, you are told, with the price, before he starts.

How old can a water heater get?

Many insurers reckon a tank lasts around 20 years. If yours is older, the insurer may reduce the payout if it leaks. The rating plate usually shows the year of manufacture. Take a photo of it and send it with your request.

Why do you ask for photos of the rating plate and the floor?

The rating plate tells us the size, power and age. The photo of the floor shows whether there is a drain and a leak stop where the tank stands. Then Eirik can say whether the fixed price holds, and bring the right tank and parts the first time.

Does there have to be a floor drain where the tank stands?

Current regulations require that leaks from the tank can be detected and led away, for example with a floor drain, a leak stop or a watertight base. If that is missing, Eirik suggests the simplest solution that meets the requirements, and you get the price before he starts.

Do I need an electrician?

If the tank is plugged into an ordinary socket, Eirik can usually manage without. If it is hard-wired, an electrician must disconnect and reconnect it. Eirik can arrange that for you.

How long will we be without hot water?

The replacement itself usually takes a working day or less. After that it takes a few hours for the new tank to heat the water. Cold water is back on as soon as the tank is connected.

Can I buy the tank myself?

Talk to Eirik first. The fixed price assumes the tank fits and has the right connections. If you buy it yourself, you are responsible for the tank itself, and extra work because it doesn't fit is charged at the hourly rate.

    Leaking right now?

    1. Turn off the main stopcock. It is usually where the water comes into the house: in the cellar, the utility room, under the kitchen sink or by the water heater. If you have a well, you can also switch off the pump.
    2. Switch off the power to the water heater at the fuse box if the tank is leaking or the water is turned off.
    3. Mop up what you can, and take photos of the damage and of what is leaking.
    4. Report it to your insurer. Most have their own emergency service that can come and dry things out.
    5. Call Eirik.

    Eirik takes urgent jobs when he can, but Kobber og Kran does not have a 24-hour emergency service. If he can't answer, your insurer's emergency service is the quickest help.
