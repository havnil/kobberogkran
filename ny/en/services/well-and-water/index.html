Price before we start

Many people in Aurskog-Høland have their own well. It gives good water when everything works, but the pump, pressure tank and filter need looking after. Eirik helps with everything from the well into the house, in homes, cabins and barns.

What Eirik helps with

  • Pump and pressure tank: the pump runs all the time, starts and stops often, or the pressure is low
  • Brown water and stains: iron and manganese that build up in basins and laundry
  • Smell: water that smells of rotten eggs or earth
  • Bacteria: UV systems when the water test shows they are needed
  • Filters and servicing: replacing filter media, cartridges and UV lamps
  • Farms: water in barns, drinking bowls and frost-free water posts in yards and pastures

The water test comes first

The right water treatment starts with knowing what is in the water. An iron filter doesn’t help against bacteria, and a UV system doesn’t remove smells. If you have a water test, send it. If not, Eirik can tell you what it should test for.

What Eirik needs from you

  • Whether it is a drilled well or a dug well
  • A photo of the pump and pressure tank, ideally with the gauge
  • What you notice: low pressure, brown water, a smell or the pump running all the time
  • Whether the water has been tested, and the result if you have it

Barns and farms

Water for animals can’t wait until spring. Eirik lays and repairs water pipes in barns, fits drinking bowls and installs frost-free water posts, so you have water in the yard and on the pasture when it is cold too. Read more about water for farming.

Partners

Well drilling and digging are done by regular partners. If the water needs treatment, Eirik works with a supplier of water treatment systems. He arranges the contact, so you do not have to search yourself.

Questions and answers

Common questions

The pump runs all the time. What is wrong?

It may be a leak somewhere on the pipe, a pressure tank that has lost its air pressure, or a pressure switch that is set wrongly. The pump wears out quickly when it keeps starting and stopping. Take photos of the pump, the pressure tank and the gauge, and send them with your request.

The water is brown or leaves stains. What could it be?

Brown water is often caused by iron or manganese in the well water, which is common in this area. It is usually solved with a filter. The water should be tested first, so the filter is right and no more expensive than it needs to be.

The water smells of rotten eggs. Is it dangerous?

The smell usually comes from hydrogen sulphide, often found in drilled wells. In normal amounts it is more unpleasant than dangerous, but it can corrode pipes and equipment. There are filters and aeration systems that remove it. A water test shows what is needed.

How do I test the water?

You order sample bottles from an accredited laboratory and send the sample back following their instructions. The municipality or the Norwegian Food Safety Authority can recommend laboratories. If you already have an analysis, send it with your request.

Do I need a UV system?

UV light kills bacteria in the water and is common where the test shows bacteria, for example in dug wells. It doesn't remove iron, manganese or smells, so it is often combined with a filter. The lamp needs replacing regularly, usually once a year.

Do you help with water in the barn?

Yes. Eirik does water pipes and drinking bowls in barns, frost-free water posts in the yard and on pasture, and pressure systems for farms. Farms out here need water that works in January too.

Do you drill wells?

No, but Eirik works with a well driller and arranges the contact. He does everything from the well and in: pump, pressure tank, filter, UV and the pipes in the house.

What does it cost?

Troubleshooting and servicing are charged at the hourly rate. For a new filter, UV system or pump you get an estimate or a fixed price once Eirik knows what is needed. See the prices page for the hourly rate and service van.

    Leaking right now?

    1. Turn off the main stopcock. It is usually where the water comes into the house: in the cellar, the utility room, under the kitchen sink or by the water heater. If you have a well, you can also switch off the pump.
    2. Switch off the power to the water heater at the fuse box if the tank is leaking or the water is turned off.
    3. Mop up what you can, and take photos of the damage and of what is leaking.
    4. Report it to your insurer. Most have their own emergency service that can come and dry things out.
    5. Call Eirik.

    Eirik takes urgent jobs when he can, but Kobber og Kran does not have a 24-hour emergency service. If he can't answer, your insurer's emergency service is the quickest help.
